Why I Am Building an Applied AI Learning Hub

Published 20 July 2026 · Updated 20 July 2026

I am building the Applied AI Learning Hub as a public record of a deliberate career transition: from long-standing solution architecture work toward data science, machine learning, deep learning, and applied AI engineering. The site is meant to be useful to visitors, but it is primarily a tool for disciplined learning.

From architecture to applied AI

For more than a decade I have worked in solution architecture across software systems, Java services, enterprise integrations, SQL, and cloud platforms. That background taught me how to frame problems, design for change, and communicate technical trade-offs. What it did not automatically provide is fluency in modern machine learning practice.

I am not discarding architecture experience. I am extending it. Systems thinking remains valuable when models move from notebooks into real environments with data quality issues, integration constraints, monitoring needs, and organisational accountability.

Why I am studying applied AI

Interest alone is not enough for a credible transition. Formal study creates structure, feedback, and depth. The Master Programme in Applied Artificial Intelligence at Luleå University of Technology (2026–2028, distance learning) gives me a sustained path through programming for machine learning, AI foundations, pattern recognition, and later deep learning and research work.

Alongside the programme I am practising Python and building public-safe projects. My current level is beginner-to-intermediate in Python and early in machine learning. That honesty matters. Overstating skill creates fragile confidence and weak portfolios.

Why documenting learning helps

Writing forces precision. When I explain a concept in public language, gaps become visible quickly. A learning hub also creates continuity: course notes, project decisions, and reflections stay connected instead of disappearing into private folders.

Documentation is also a professional signal. It shows process, judgement, and growth over time — especially important when changing direction after years in another specialism.

How this site will evolve

This first version establishes the architecture of the hub: course catalogue, project showcase, roadmap, resources, and blog. Content will expand as coursework and projects progress. Status labels such as planned, upcoming, in progress, and completed are intentional. Nothing is marked finished unless it is finished.

The technical stack is intentionally simple — HTML, CSS, JavaScript, and JSON — so the site can run on GitHub Pages today and migrate later if a framework becomes useful.

Why practical projects matter

Courses provide foundations. Projects reveal whether those foundations can be applied. I plan to document public dataset work, evaluation practice, and eventually more advanced applied AI systems. Each project should include limitations and lessons, not only polished outcomes.

Responsible AI from the start

Applied AI is not only model accuracy. Privacy, consent, security, and misuse risks belong in the learning process early, especially for anyone with experience near sensitive domains. This site itself follows that principle: no student credentials, no private employer material, and no confidential health or personal data.

A personal site, not an official LTU resource

This website is a personal learning portfolio. It is not an official website of Luleå University of Technology and does not represent university policy, teaching materials, or endorsement. Official programme information should always be verified through university sources.
