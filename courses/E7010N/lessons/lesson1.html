Lesson 1: Course Introduction

Artificial Intelligence and Business Development

Business value · Industrial B2B firms · Five course themes · Research-based analysis · Reflection reports · Group project
01

What this course is about

E7010N studies artificial intelligence from a business development and organizational perspective. The central question is how firms can use AI to create and capture value: for example, by improving internal operations, developing a new offering, changing a business model or working with partners in an ecosystem.

The course is designed for students with different backgrounds, including Applied AI and International Business. It does not teach how to build or train AI models. Instead, it examines the business and organizational conditions around AI and asks how technical possibilities can become useful outcomes for a firm and its customers.

AI capability+Organization and business model+Partners and context→Business value

The introduction frames this as a social and technical challenge: technology matters, but people, processes, business models and relationships with other organizations influence whether AI can be scaled and create value.

02

From using AI to creating value

The opening presentation contrasts the growing use of AI in organizations with the smaller share that report substantial business impact. It also highlights that obstacles to scaling AI often involve people and processes, not only the algorithms. The point is not that firms should stop experimenting. It is that adoption by itself does not guarantee useful or profitable results.

Think like a business manager

For each AI initiative, ask what changes for the customer or the firm, how that value will be delivered, and who must contribute to make it work. A technically capable system may still fail to create value if it does not fit the work process, solve a meaningful problem, or connect to a viable way of delivering and capturing value.

QuestionWhat to examine
Where could AI help?A customer need, an internal process, a product or service, or a business opportunity or threat.
Can the firm make it work?Organizational readiness, capabilities, workflows, people and resources needed to use AI.
How will value be created and captured?The value proposition, delivery arrangement, revenue or cost effects, and who benefits.
Who else matters?Partners and other ecosystem actors needed to build, deliver, scale or govern the solution.

Course lens: examples focus mainly on industrial business-to-business (B2B) firms. The course is not centered on consumer apps or technical model development, although students can connect the ideas to their own interests where the course themes fit.

03

The five themes

The five themes were selected from recurring topics in research and from discussions with companies. Each theme is supported by readings in Canvas, including peer-reviewed research and practitioner publications. The themes are connected: the first four inform one another, while managing threats matters across all of them.

THEME 01

Leveraging AI business opportunities

Identify where AI may create business value and what an opportunity means in a specific firm context.

THEME 02

Assessing business readiness for AI

Examine whether a firm has the conditions and capabilities needed to use AI and capture value from it.

THEME 03

Business model innovation and AI

Explore how AI can change how a firm proposes, delivers and captures value.

THEME 04

Expanding AI boundaries: the ecosystem

Consider which partners and relationships help a firm develop and scale AI-enabled value.

THEME 05 · CROSS-CUTTING

The dark side of AI: managing business threats

Examine threats and challenges that can affect opportunities, readiness, business models and ecosystems.

Opportunity↔Readiness↔Business model↔Ecosystem

Managing AI-related business threats applies across all four.

The introductory lecture presents these as a map for the course. The later theme lectures and readings develop each topic in more depth.

04

What you should learn

By the end of the course, students should be able to understand and examine concepts and theories used to analyze, assess, implement and commercialize AI-driven solutions in industrial firms. The three intended learning outcomes are:

Learning outcomeWhat it means in practice
Understand the research frontierRecognize important current trends in AI and business development.
Discuss and critically analyzeCompare concepts, assumptions, models and frameworks from scientific and practitioner sources, and explain how they relate.
Examine practical implicationsApply course insights to a firm, evaluate how current trends affect it, and communicate conclusions in speech and writing.

Teaching combines lectures with practical company examples and in-class activities. Students are expected to participate, discuss cases, apply ideas to business situations and critically examine the concepts and frameworks in the readings.

05

How the course is assessed

There is no final course exam. The grade is based on Canvas quizzes, individual reflection reports and a group final project with both a group presentation and an individual defence.

AssessmentPointsFormat

Final project report45Group report giving theoretically grounded, practical guidance to an industrial B2B firm.
Group presentation5Present the project’s analysis and recommendations.

Total100

Final grades are based on total points: U for 0–54, 3 for 55–69, 4 for 70–89 and 5 for 90–100.

Quizzes

There are five Canvas quizzes based on the lecture material and related articles. Each quiz is individual, allows one timed attempt, and is open for one week after its lecture. The lecturer emphasizes completing it before it closes; missed quizzes are not reopened.

06

Writing a reflection report

Choose three of the five themes and write one report for each. Each report is worth part of the 15-point total and should be no longer than two pages, using 12-point Times New Roman and 1.5 line spacing.

A reflection report should combine the readings into an argument. It should not give a separate, paper-by-paper summary. Use the sources to explain what you learned about the theme, assess the strength and relevance of their ideas, and connect theory to implications for practice.

MoveQuestion to answer
State a purposeWhat question or issue will guide this reflection?
Compare and integrateWhat key messages do the papers share or disagree about, and how do their approaches differ?
Evaluate criticallyAre the claims convincing? What evidence supports them, and what assumptions or limits should be considered?
Connect to practiceWhat example shows why the ideas matter for a firm or its stakeholders?
Conclude clearlyWhat insight answers the purpose you stated at the beginning?

An optional early submission is available for feedback and is not graded. The same report can then be submitted for grading by the required deadline.

07

The final project: practical guidance for a firm

In a group, select an industrial B2B firm that is working with AI. Identify an AI-related opportunity or challenge that matters for business value, then use course ideas and additional research to develop guidance for the firm. Examples may involve internal process improvement, predictive maintenance, a digital business model or a commercialized AI-enabled offering.

The project is not intended to be a detailed technical description of an AI system. Its focus is the business problem, the relevant theory, what the analysis implies for the firm, and what managers should do.

How the report develops

VersionMain purposeWhat the group develops
1 · Set the sceneDefine and justify the project focus.Introduce the firm and AI solution; define the practical opportunity or challenge; connect it to a course theme and relevant theory; state a clear project purpose.
2 · Review and frameworkBuild a theory-based way to analyze the problem.Update from feedback; explain how literature was searched; synthesize the most relevant findings; develop a framework, process, criteria or guidelines for the firm context.
3 · RecommendationsTurn the analysis into practical advice.Update from feedback; develop specific recommendations and an implementation plan, including steps, timing and resources; state the conclusions.
Final reportPresent a complete, persuasive analysis.Incorporate feedback; add a front page, executive summary and APA-style references; submit a focused report of 6,000–8,000 words, excluding references.

The first three versions are development stages with supervisory feedback; the final report is graded. The final work should combine course readings with at least ten additional sources found through the group’s own search. It should offer a clear framework and recommendations that fit the selected firm, rather than simply restating what the literature says.

Firm challenge or opportunity→Research and course concepts→Context-specific framework→Recommendations and implementation

Presentation and individual defence

The group presents a synthesis of its project and receives feedback. Each member must contribute and be ready to answer questions about any part of the report. The individual defence assesses whether each student understands the full project, not only the section they personally wrote.

08

Group work and participation

The course brings together students from different programs and includes both campus and distance participation. Different backgrounds can strengthen the project, but groups need to agree early on roles, writing responsibilities and a communication channel. The instructor describes group formation after registration and asks students to use the course communication channels and Canvas for confirmed arrangements.

  • All submissions and presentations are in English.
  • Supervisory meetings are online and attendance is compulsory.
  • Attendance is also compulsory for the final project presentation.
  • Groups should meet outside class as needed to complete the project.

Check Canvas and the current study guide for confirmed group information, submission dates and activity instructions.

09

Key points from the introduction

  • The course asks how firms can create and capture business value with AI.
  • Its main perspective is organizational and managerial, with an industrial B2B focus.
  • The five themes move from opportunity and readiness through business model innovation and ecosystems, with threats affecting every theme.
  • Readings and lectures provide concepts; quizzes check understanding, reflection reports develop critical analysis, and the project applies ideas to a firm.
  • The project should produce context-specific guidance: a clear problem, a research-based framework, practical recommendations and an implementation plan.

A useful starting question for your project: Which AI-related opportunity or challenge matters to a particular industrial B2B firm, why is it important, and what can research help the firm decide or do?
